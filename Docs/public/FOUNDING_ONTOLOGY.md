## Founding statement

We don’t build monuments to what we know. We build instruments for what we don’t yet understand.

The Cenotaph is a threshold, not a trophy. The Atlas is the place of exploration. An instrument is where a question can be touched. The visitor is the observer.

## The question under the Atlas

How much of what we experience is the world itself, and how much is the model through which we perceive it?

That question informs the fair. It is not a result.

## Wonder and evidence

Wonder comes before explanation. It is allowed to arrive first.

Wonder is not evidence. Atmosphere is not evidence. An artistic analogy is not an experiment. A beautiful picture of a model is still a picture of a model.

A visitor does not have to accept a worldview to use an instrument. The invitation is to find where that worldview stops working.

## Four epistemic layers

Keep these distinct in public copy and in the instrument itself.

1. **Observation.** What was actually measured or perceived, and by what procedure.
2. **Model.** The mathematical or conceptual representation. Name the assumptions it requires.
3. **Interpretation.** The meaning someone draws from the model. An interpretation can be revised without pretending the measurements changed.
4. **Open question.** What is unresolved, untested, or only speculative.

Humility rules:

- An observation is not automatically an explanation.
- A model is not the world.
- A mathematical possibility is not necessarily a physical possibility.
- An artistic analogy is not experimental evidence.
- A speculative hypothesis is not an established result.

Scientific humility is what makes ambitious exploration inspectable.

## Simulation is not a physical experiment

An interactive instrument may be a mathematical model, a simulation, a perceptual demonstration, or an exploratory visualization.

Say which one it is. Do not write as if every ride took a laboratory measurement. Where a real measurement is shown, cite it and keep it separate from the model that follows.

The visitor is free to challenge the model: change one thing, look again, and ask what the representation hid.

## Two sequences, one fair

These are complementary descriptions of a visit. They are not two navigation systems.

- Wonder → Observe → Model → Interpret → Question again.
- Ride → Map → Thought path.

A child should be able to enter because something looks fascinating. A scientist should be able to stay because the assumptions, limits, and evidence can be inspected.

## Homepage voice

The threshold keeps one question as the title: *There is always another why.*

*Change one thing. Look again.* is the operational line of the instruments.

The founding statement sits beneath the threshold, as the passage into the register. It does not replace the question, and it does not become a second hero headline.

Open instruments are named by their questions. Projects that are not released stay on the horizon and do not receive a public route.

## What this document is not

It is not a manifesto to be pasted in full onto the homepage.
It is not permission to add a second WebGL world, a new Atlas control, or a new instrument under the cover of philosophy.
It is not an equivalence between a metaphor and a theory.
